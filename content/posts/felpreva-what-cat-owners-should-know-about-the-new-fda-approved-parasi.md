The FDA approved Felpreva on November 16, 2026, and if you share your home with a cat, this is worth paying attention to. Virbac's new prescription topical is the first product of its kind cleared for use in American cats: a single spot-on [that covers](/pet-insurance-that-covers-pre-existing-conditions-options/) fleas, ticks, ear mites, roundworms, and tapeworms all at once, applied just four times a year. That's a meaningful departure from the current reality for most cat owners, who piece together separate monthly flea treatments, periodic deworming, and the occasional vet visit to address whatever parasite slipped through.

The timing matters. Veterinary Practice News reported the approval on November 16, 2026, the same day the FDA made it official, and Virbac's own press release confirmed the product is being positioned for U.S. distributor rollout in 2027. So Felpreva isn't something you can pick up this week, but it's close enough that understanding what it is, who it's for, and what it actually does will help you have a smarter conversation with your vet when it does arrive.

According to Today's Veterinary Business, Felpreva combines three active ingredients: emodepside and praziquantel (which handle the internal parasites) and tigolaner (the external parasite component). Tigolaner is notable because it belongs to a bispyrazole chemical class, which represents a genuinely new mechanism of action for flea, tick, and mite control in cats. That matters clinically, particularly as resistance to older compound classes continues to develop.

## What Felpreva Actually Covers (and What It Doesn't)

The broadest selling point is the parasite range. One application handles:

| Parasite | Coverage Duration |
| --- | --- |
| Fleas | 12 weeks |
| Black-legged ticks | 12 weeks |
| Lone star ticks | 8 weeks |
| Mites | Per application |
| Roundworms | Per application |
| Tapeworms | Per application |

That's four doses per year total. The math works out to roughly one application every 13 weeks for the flea and black-legged tick coverage, with the lone star tick protection running slightly shorter at 8 weeks per dose.

What Felpreva does not cover: heartworm. If your cat is in a heartworm-endemic region, that's a separate conversation with your vet. Felpreva also doesn't cover hookworms. For cats who go outdoors in areas with high hookworm exposure, that gap is worth discussing explicitly when your vet is evaluating whether Felpreva fits your specific cat's risk profile.

## Who Can Actually Use This Product

The FDA approval specifies cats at least 6 months of age and weighing at least 2.2 pounds. It's feline-only. There is no dog formulation, and given how differently cats and dogs metabolize certain compounds (the emodepside component is a good example), this isn't a product you'd ever improvise across species.

Prescription status means you can't buy this at the pet store or online without a veterinary prescription. That's not a bureaucratic inconvenience. It reflects the fact that combining internal and external parasite control in one product, at quarterly intervals, requires a vet to assess your individual cat's health status, weight, and existing medications before recommending it.

If your cat has a history of neurological issues or takes other medications, bring a full medication list to that appointment. Emodepside in particular has interactions worth reviewing.

## The EU Track Record

Felpreva has been sold in Europe since its EU authorization, giving American [cat owners](/new-world-screwworm-in-us-pets-what-dog-and-cat-owners-must-know/) a body of real-world data beyond the controlled clinical trials that supported FDA approval. That four-year head start in those markets gives American cat owners something useful: a population of cats who've already received the product, and a body of real-world data beyond the controlled clinical trials that supported FDA approval.

This doesn't mean every concern is settled. Post-market surveillance in the EU has continued, as it does with any veterinary pharmaceutical. But the EU availability does mean that the product isn't brand-new to the world when it arrives in U.S. clinics, and your vet may already have access to European clinical literature if you want to look deeper.

## What This Might Mean for Your Parasite Control Budget

Thinking about annual costs is legitimate here. A quarterly prescription topical will likely carry a higher per-dose price than individual monthly generics, though the actual U.S. retail pricing hasn't been publicly set as of late November 2026. The honest calculation requires looking at what you're currently spending across all the separate products Felpreva would replace: the monthly flea preventive, the separate tick product if you use one, deworming treatments, and vet visits tied to parasite-related problems.

If you want a clearer picture of your overall annual pet costs before making decisions about premium prescription products, the [Cost Owning Dog Cat](/cost-of-owning-a-dog-cat/) tool can help you model those numbers more realistically for a cat.

The prescription requirement also means a [vet visit](/your-dogs-first-vet-visit-guide/) is part of the upfront cost. For cats who are already seen annually, that may be baked in. For cats who only see a vet when something's wrong, budget for the exam.

## When to Ask Your Vet About Felpreva

Right now, in late 2026, the most practical step is simply being informed. When Felpreva becomes available through U.S. distributors in 2027, your vet may proactively bring it up, or you may need to ask. Either way, the right questions are:

Does my cat's parasite risk profile match what Felpreva covers? Are there any contraindications given my cat's age, weight, health status, or current medications? And what does the quarterly schedule look like practically, in terms of cost and timing for my cat specifically?

Don't walk in asking for a specific product. Walk in prepared to describe your cat's lifestyle (indoor only, indoor/outdoor, how much time outside, regional tick exposure) and let the conversation develop from there. That gives your vet the actual information they need.

Felpreva won't be right for every cat. For cats with complex health histories, or those whose parasite exposure is genuinely low, the existing product lineup may still make more sense. But for owners who've struggled to keep up with multiple monthly treatments, or whose cats have ongoing issues with both internal and external parasites, the quarterly single-product approach is worth a real conversation when it becomes available in 2027.
