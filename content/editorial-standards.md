# How Pet Doctor Guide Is Written

Pet Doctor Guide helps pet owners understand symptoms, preventive care, nutrition and behavior, so they can make better decisions together with their veterinarian. This page explains, plainly, how the articles are made.

## How articles are made

Articles on Pet Doctor Guide are drafted with AI writing tools. Each draft starts from public reference sources on the topic, including those listed below, and from recent news when a topic is changing.

Before an article is published, a second automated pass reviews it and removes or rewrites anything it cannot stand behind: statistics, prices or dates it is not confident are correct, studies or organizations that may be misattributed, and personal stories presented as if they happened to the writer.

Articles are not reviewed by a veterinarian before publication. Use them as background for a conversation with one, not as a substitute.

The site owner audits published articles in batches, merging pages that overlap, deleting weak ones and correcting errors.

## Who writes here

The bylines on Pet Doctor Guide (Amanda Foster, James Whitfield, Karen Lopez, Michelle Chen, Rachel Sanders, Tom Harris) are pen names, one for each area of coverage. They are not real individuals, they hold no professional licenses, and their profile images are illustrations, not photographs of real people.

## Sources we rely on

- [American Veterinary Medical Association](https://www.avma.org/): Veterinary guidance
- [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/): Pet health and safety
- [FDA: Animal & Veterinary](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary): Pet food and drug safety
- [Cornell University College of Veterinary Medicine](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/): Veterinary research

Where an article depends on a specific figure, rule or study, it links to or names the source so you can check it.

## Limits of this site

Nothing here can diagnose or treat your pet. If your pet may have eaten something toxic, call your veterinarian, an emergency clinic or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435 (a consultation fee may apply). Trouble breathing, collapse, seizures, a swollen or hard belly, repeated vomiting and straining to urinate are emergencies: go to a vet now rather than reading further.

## Corrections

If you find an error, please report it through the [contact page](/contact/). Reports go directly to the site owner, and confirmed errors are corrected in the article.

## How the site makes money

Pet Doctor Guide may earn money from display advertising and from affiliate links, including Amazon links (as an Amazon Associate, the site earns from qualifying purchases). Advertisers and affiliate partners do not see or approve articles before they are published.
